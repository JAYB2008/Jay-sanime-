# App Type Playbooks

Use these patterns to make each product type feel specifically designed instead of generic.

## Boba, cafe, food ordering, and delivery apps

Design goals: appetite appeal, speed, trust, customization clarity, and repeat ordering.

- Lead with large product photography or illustrated drink cards.
- Use warm, creamy, tea-inspired, fruit-inspired, or premium dessert palettes depending on brand.
- Use rounded product cards with visible price, rating/popularity, tags, and quick add.
- Make customization feel fun and clear: size, ice, sugar, toppings, milk, tea base, temperature.
- Show cart state persistently on mobile with a bottom checkout bar.
- Include pickup/delivery toggle, ETA, store status, rewards points, favorites, and reorder shortcuts.
- Use real icons for cart, rewards, location, search, filters, plus/add, favorite, and order status.
- Empty cart should suggest popular drinks or previous favorites.

## SaaS dashboards

Design goals: confidence, clarity, speed, and actionability.

- Use a persistent sidebar or topbar depending on destination count.
- Use a page header with title, summary, date/filter controls, and primary action.
- Prioritize KPI cards, charts, tables, alerts, and recent activity based on user goals.
- Align numbers right and use tabular figures.
- Support dense information without sacrificing spacing.
- Provide filter-empty and no-data-yet states separately.

## Media, anime, streaming, and content apps

Design goals: immersion, discovery, visual richness, and watch/read continuity.

- Use dark or cinematic surfaces when appropriate.
- Lead with artwork, posters, cover cards, trailers, continue-watching rows, genres, and recommendations.
- Use horizontal carousels carefully; ensure keyboard/touch accessibility.
- Make metadata scannable: rating, episodes, duration, language, status, release date.
- Provide skeletons for image-heavy loading states.
- Keep primary actions prominent: watch, continue, save, download, share.

## Ecommerce and marketplaces

Design goals: product confidence, comparison, conversion, and trust.

- Prioritize imagery, price, options, reviews, delivery/return trust signals, and clear checkout path.
- Use filters and sorting near product grids.
- Make product cards consistent but informative.
- Keep add-to-cart and buy actions visually distinct.
- Include inventory, shipping, discount, and variant states.

## Social, chat, and community apps

Design goals: immediacy, identity, conversation flow, and safety.

- Use avatars, names, timestamps, presence indicators, message states, and unread badges.
- Keep composer actions within thumb reach on mobile.
- Make moderation/report/block actions discoverable but not visually dominant.
- Empty states should help users find people, start chats, or join communities.

## Creator tools and editors

Design goals: focus, power, discoverability, and predictable control.

- Use canvas/workspace layouts with side panels, inspectors, toolbars, and command menus.
- Preserve space for the actual creative object.
- Use keyboard shortcuts and tooltips for repeated actions.
- Distinguish selection, hover, active tool, and disabled states clearly.

## Finance, wallet, and analytics apps

Design goals: trust, precision, risk clarity, and calm decision-making.

- Use restrained palettes and strong contrast.
- Use tabular numbers and clear positive/negative status treatment.
- Clearly distinguish balances, pending activity, completed transactions, and risk warnings.
- Confirm irreversible actions and explain fees/statuses.

## Learning, onboarding, and habit apps

Design goals: motivation, progress, low anxiety, and next-step clarity.

- Use progress indicators, milestones, friendly illustrations/icons, and clear next actions.
- Break complex setup into steps.
- Celebrate success without blocking the user's next action.
- Empty states should teach the value of the feature.
